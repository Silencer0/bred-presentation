## Speaking notes

1. We ask whether paying by cash or UPI changes which everyday purchases students remember several days later. This is a proposed field experiment, not a report of findings.

2. Tracking a weekly total requires remembering many small purchases. Cash handling may make the payment memorable. UPI also supplies histories and notifications that may rehearse the purchase. These are competing predictions, not conclusions.

3. Soman (2001) connects payment rehearsal and immediacy to memory and later spending. Gafeeva, Hoelzl and Roschk (2018, published online 2017) compare recall of recent cash and card payments; recall was poorer for cards, and non-payment usage patterns mattered. Those are card findings, not direct UPI evidence. Srivastava and Raghubir (2002) study memory-based expense estimates and decomposition. Our specific gap is delayed everyday UPI purchase recall assessed against independent outlet records. Avoid the stronger unsupported claim that no such research exists anywhere.

4. H1 is two-sided: purchase recall rates differ between assigned modes. H0 is no difference. Salience predicts a cash advantage. Records and rehearsal predict a UPI advantage. The study does not prespecify that UPI must be worse.

5. Recruit consenting adult hostel residents through wing representatives and campus lists. This is campus convenience recruitment, not random population sampling. Eligibility requires both payment modes and at least 25% habitual cash use. Match wings on baseline purchase-memory performance, Spendthrift–Tightwad score, cash habit, year, gender mix, size and outlet distance. Randomize one wing in each pair to each mode with concealed allocation. Target 44 wings, 22 pairs, about ten participants per wing. Whole-wing assignment reduces within-wing contamination; it cannot eliminate cross-wing friendships or shared meals. Do not discard people to create balance.

6. Announce ₹500 in advance as payment for Part 2 tasks. Cash enters the person's wallet without an envelope; UPI enters their account. Neither arm has a separate spending pot. The five partner outlets include food outlets and stationery. Both arms show the same Wellness Week discount card for 5% off. Assigned mode applies only there. People may spend elsewhere and need not exhaust the ₹500. UPI history and notifications remain part of the treatment. Collect no personal transaction histories.

7. Pilot first on two wings outside the main sample. Week 0 baseline includes a shopping-video memory task, not RAVLT. Week 1 matches and assigns wings. At the Sunday kickoff, participants complete tasks, receive payment and get discount cards. Monday–Friday, staff scan cards and log time, items, paid amount and mode. Daily till/merchant-statement reconciliation and dummy-card audits check completeness. There are no daily participant check-ins. On Sunday at 6 pm, everyone attends parallel rooms simultaneously. Phones are collected. Fixed recall order: initial total, purchase count, free-recall purchase list, day-by-day reconstruction, revised total. Free recall supplies the primary outcome. Process checks, price knowledge, suspicion and available screen-time/app-usage measures follow recall. Debrief after all sessions, with an option to withdraw data.

8. For participants with logged purchases, recall rate is correctly listed purchases divided by logged purchases. Match outlet and item category, allowing the stated ±1-day date tolerance. Amount accuracy is separate. Two condition-blind coders score matches and report agreement. The five-purchase, three-recalled figure is a worked example only. A zero-purchase participant has no defined recall rate, not 0% or 100%. Report non-purchase rates by arm and retain other defined outcomes. The selection issue requires a prespecified analysis and sensitivity strategy.

9. H2 compares absolute total-estimate error and signed underestimation. Absolute error is |log((recalled + 10)/(actual + 10))|. Signed error is the same log ratio without the absolute value; negative means underestimation. The ₹10 offset is a protocol choice that keeps this outcome defined at zero spending. False recall counts listed purchases with no logged match. H3 compares the gain after day-by-day reconstruction, explicitly exploratory because cueing, retesting and anchoring are inseparable under fixed order. H4 examines app use, screen time and habitual cash share. App-use and screen-time associations do not establish causal mechanisms. App-open metrics are used only where the device reports them.

10. Analyze by assigned mode. The primary purchase-level logistic mixed model includes mode, purchase delay, baseline memory, tightwad and cash share, wing-pair fixed effects, and participant/wing random intercepts. Report odds ratio, 95% CI and a recall-rate contrast. Do not adjust the primary analysis for spending, purchase count or app checking as if they were baseline confounders. The sample graph reproduces protocol planning scenarios: ICC .03 needs about 356, .05 about 407, .10 about 533. They assume ten people per wing, d=.30, 80% power, alpha=.05 and a 20% variance reduction from matching/covariates. These are approximations, not a verified power calculation for the binary mixed model. The proposal targets about 440 and recruits about 520; confirm participants-per-wing and attrition assumptions with simulation before launch. Add matched pairs if needed. One primary test; Holm correction for the H2 family; H3/H4 exploratory. Missing-data imputation and bounds need their assumptions specified.

11. This is a comparison of real payment regimes with different record access. It cannot isolate a pure encoding mechanism. Scope is one campus, users with at least 25% cash use, small purchases and five weekdays. Staff see payment mode; follow-up assessors and coders can be blinded. Fixed cue order limits H3. Zero purchases and attrition may select the purchases contributing to the model. Ethics approval, disclosed purchase logging, partial-disclosure consent, faculty oversight, secure minimal data, debriefing and withdrawal rights precede collection. Screen-time screenshots should exclude unrelated sensitive content and follow approved consent.

12. If there is a mode difference, replicate and then experimentally vary record access or notifications to test mechanisms. Longer windows, larger purchases and other populations test generalization. With no clear difference, inspect the CI: a precise near-zero estimate is more informative than a wide interval, but nonsignificance alone does not establish equivalence. Opposing mechanisms are a possible explanation to investigate, not proof that they cancel. A future variant can control record access or extend delays.

## Five-minute Q&A preparation

- Why wings? Reduce within-wing contamination while retaining incidental recall. Clustering changes effective sample size and must be modeled.
- Why not a crossover? A second memory test would no longer be surprising.
- Why not match on RAVLT? The revised design uses a purchase-memory task to match the target construct; pilot reliability remains necessary.
- What happens to people who check UPI? They remain in their assigned group. Checking is part of real UPI, not an exclusion rule.
- Is the primary outcome defined for everyone? No. Zero-purchase cases are reported separately, with the resulting selection limitation made explicit.
- Are mechanism results causal? No. App use and screen time are observational after assignment. Randomized mechanism manipulations would be needed.
- Does the sample guarantee 80% power? No. It is an approximate planning target; the actual clustered binary design needs simulation and realistic ICC/attrition assumptions.
- What is the indicative budget? About ₹2.8 lakh for the design-only proposal, including ₹2.2 lakh in Part 2 payments. Reconcile the recruitment/attrition allowance before implementation.
- Do Lee bounds solve arbitrary missingness? No. They require assumptions such as monotone selection. State and assess those assumptions; do not describe them as assumption-free worst-case bounds.

## Critical evaluation of the two other teams

Use the allotted 2.5 minutes for one focused question, then listen and offer one feasible improvement. Record a strength and a concern for each of the two teams.

Useful questions:

1. What exactly was randomized, and what causal comparison does that assignment identify?
2. How is your main construct operationalized, and is there independent ground truth?
3. Could exclusions, missing outcomes or post-assignment controls change the comparison?
4. If the finding is null, which effect sizes would the confidence interval still allow?

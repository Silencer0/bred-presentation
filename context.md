## Literature review and variables

Added 17 November 2026 after TA feedback that the review was too thin and lacked evidence that cash payers remember purchases better. Three rows, two studies each:

- **Cash payers recall spending better:** Gafeeva, Hoelzl & Roschk (2017, *Marketing Letters*; cafeteria field study, n = 496, cash payers recalled the amount just paid more accurately than card payers) and Soman (2001).
- **Why cash feels different:** Raghubir & Srivastava (2008, payment transparency and form) and Ceravolo et al. (2019, fMRI pilot, n = 16, cash vs card vs smartphone).
- **Recall methods and the UPI gap:** Srivastava & Raghubir (2002) and Dev et al. (2024, arXiv preprint / CHI 2024 Late-Breaking Work; about 75% self-reported more spending under UPI, with no recall measured).

The Gafeeva paper is the same one previously cited as 2018. It appeared online in 2017 (vol. 29, 2018). Prelec & Loewenstein (1998) was the alternative pain-of-paying source and was not added. References are now alphabetical, with course materials last.

## Recruitment and allocation

- One college only; eligible adult newcomers who can use both cash and UPI.
- Recruitment is voluntary convenience recruitment from the accessible cohort. Newcomer eligibility restricts prior campus price knowledge.
- Pair participants using baseline memory, continuous spending tendency and habitual cash use.
- Use Mahalanobis distance on their within-sample standardized scores, then optimal nonbipartite matching using `nbpMatching`.
- If the cohort is odd, the participant whose best available match is most distant remains outside the paired primary analysis, but still participates and is considered in a sensitivity analysis. Their assignment procedure needs specifying before a mode-based sensitivity analysis.
- Flag and report pairs above the 95th percentile of matching distance.
- A computer coin flip assigns one person per pair to cash and the other to UPI. Allocation stays concealed until Sunday.
- Check standardized mean differences on the three matching measures. If any exceeds 0.20, rerandomize up to three times under a prespecified rule.
- The tracking-status and payment-history-frequency questions have been removed, along with tracking-status exact matching and the associated balance check.

**Terminology:** the team requested the heading “Matched-pair allocation.” Pair formation is deliberate, but the retained computer coin flip makes assignment *within pairs* randomized. Do not describe this as entirely non-random assignment unless that procedure is explicitly changed.

Use full measure names on slides. Do not reintroduce the spending-scale abbreviation or letter/number codes for the matching variables. Conventional test names and hypothesis labels remain in use.

## Baseline session and measures

Saturday, group, on paper, budgeted at about **55 minutes**. The team explicitly chose to preserve a **30-minute delay after the complex-figure copy ends**, extending the originally proposed session.

| Block | Duration | Procedure |
|---|---:|---|
| Verbal learning | 10 min | Five recorded-audio learning trials, fixed-time written recall after each. Announce later recall. |
| Complex-figure copy | 5 min | Copy the figure; announce later recall. |
| Questionnaire | 20 min | Personality, spending tendency, sleep, cash-use and other decoys. |
| Neutral buffer | 10 min | Preserve the figure-recall delay without memory rehearsal. |
| Delayed figure recall | 5 min | Begin 30 minutes after the copy ends. |
| Delayed verbal recall | 2 min | Record as a covariate, not in the matching composite. |

These blocks total 52 minutes. Record actual copy-to-recall and verbal-recall intervals.

### Baseline memory

Average the within-sample standardized scores for:

- Rey Auditory Verbal Learning Test, trials 1–5 total: 0–75.
- Rey–Osterrieth Complex Figure delayed recall: 0–36.

Use the learning total rather than trial five or delayed verbal recall in the composite. Two trained, blinded scorers apply the 36-point Osterrieth/Taylor scoring system to the figure; report intraclass correlation and use their average.

A low figure-copy score is a flag for possible drawing/perceptual difficulty, not an automatic exclusion. Report flagged counts. Delayed verbal recall is recorded separately as a covariate.

Pilot checks include ceiling shares at verbal-learning total ≥70 and figure delayed recall ≥34, each measure's reliability, and their correlation. Under the prespecified pilot rule, use verbal learning alone if the figure score does not predict pilot purchase recall. The exact pilot decision threshold still needs fixing.

### Spending tendency and payment habits

- Published four-item Spendthrift–Tightwad scale, total 4–26; reverse-score the specified “trouble spending” item and use the total continuously. Do not create tightwad/spendthrift categories.
- Habitual cash use: cash payments among the last ten payments, 0–10; this is the matching measure.
- Typical weekly cash-spending share checks that measure.
- Main payment app is descriptive only.

Decoys have the same visual weight as the spending scale: Ten-Item Personality Inventory (10 items), sleep (3), screen time/phone habits (3), food/diet (4), commute (2), internship expectations (3). Put the spending scale between personality and sleep items.

Report three procedural deviations: group/written verbal testing, announced delayed verbal recall and announced delayed figure recall. Announcing baseline recall avoids teaching participants to anticipate another surprise test and rehearse purchases.

## Spending period and ground truth

- Personal spending for one Monday–Sunday week at four campus outlets.
- Assigned cash-only or UPI-only condition for eligible personal purchases.
- Both groups receive an **uncapped 25% participant-ID discount**. Earlier no-discount versions are obsolete.
- Both groups retain the same completion-reward rule; the separate monetary amount and verification details have not been finalized.
- Say **participant ID**, not student ID.
- No spending cap and no participant spending diary. No minimum spending requirement.
- Vendors enter items using tablet menu buttons. Each record is one **item line**, retaining quantity: two samosas plus one chai creates two lines, not three observations.
- Fields: participant ID, outlet, item code, quantity, menu price, actual amount paid after discount, timestamp and payment mode.
- Fix item names, accepted synonyms and categories from the four menus before collection. Categories: hot drinks; cold drinks/juices; savoury snacks; sweets/bakery; meals; other.
- Reconcile records and estimate the discount budget from pilot spending.
- Report spending and item counts by mode descriptively. They are potential post-assignment outcomes, not primary-model baseline controls.

## Final recall procedure

The following **Monday at 6 pm**, digital, 20 minutes. There is no make-up session.

1. Submit and lock total amount spent and number of separate items bought.
2. Reconstruct purchases across seven dated Monday–Sunday tabs.
3. Every entry requires outlet, specific item, whole-rupee amount actually paid after discount, and confidence: Sure or Guessing.
4. Do not show menus or transaction histories; do not add an “anything else?” prompt; do not allow return to initial totals.
5. The example dish must be absent from all four menus.
6. Reject generic item-only responses such as food, meal, snack, breakfast, lunch, dinner, drink, juice, coffee, tea or something.

Confidence is separate from objective matching: a guessing entry is checked against the same record criteria. Automatically marking every guess wrong would make the requested confidence-calibration comparison uninformative.

## Scoring and analysis

### Item matching

Use a Hungarian one-to-one assignment maximizing matches between recall entries and logged item lines.

- Strict, primary: same outlet, exact item/accepted synonym, day within ±1.
- Lenient: same outlet/category, day within ±1.
- Two coders code all entries blind to payment mode after removal of mode-revealing wording. Report Cohen's kappa for item coding and day reading.

### Confirmatory outcomes

- Item recall: a binary recalled/not-recalled observation for each logged item line. Logistic mixed model with payment mode and the three baseline matching measures, plus pair and participant random intercepts. Report the mode odds ratio and average recall-probability difference.
- Total-spend error: natural log of `(initial recalled total + 10) / (actual total + 10)`.
- The second hypothesis concerns absolute spend error; the third predicts more negative signed error for UPI. Person-level models retain the baseline matching measures and pair effect.
- Holm correction across the three hypotheses.

More purchases provide more observations but also create different recall demands. Model clustering and use model-based shrunken person estimates for plots rather than ranking raw percentages. Do not automatically claim that one illustrative shrinkage calculation reproduces the fitted model.

Do not control for post-assignment purchase count in the primary model. A secondary model adding log purchase count and repeated-item status is descriptive only. Other sensitivities include equal-person weighting, lenient matching and recall above chance.

### Other measures

- Precision: matched entries / all entries.
- F1: `2 × precision × recall / (precision + recall)`.
- Recall above chance: own-log strict recall minus a prespecified chance score against other participants' logs, sampled from both conditions.
- Count error: initial count minus actual item-line count.
- Price error/bias: among strict matches, the participant's median absolute/signed recalled-minus-paid difference divided by actual paid amount.
- Day accuracy: exact-day fraction among strict matches.
- Exploratory: itemised-versus-initial spending total and Sure-versus-Guessing match-rate ratio.
- Report repeat-item versus one-off and lenient recall.

Specify donor selection/normalization for chance scores and denominator handling for zero purchases, zero entries, zero paid amounts and zero guessing match rates. Chance subtraction is a robustness measure, not a blanket guarantee that arbitrary over-listing gains nothing.

Monday nonattendance is handled with attrition reporting and Lee bounds **only when their identifying assumptions are justified**. No make-up test. The exploratory habitual-cash-use interaction remains separate from the confirmatory hypotheses.

## Ethics and interpretation

Every participant signs a detailed consent form covering tasks, logging, assignment, incentives, burden, risks, contacts and withdrawal rights. The exact hypothesis and surprise timing are partially withheld; obtain ethics approval for that withholding, then debrief and offer withdrawal of identifiable data until the stated anonymization cutoff. Do not claim approval has already been granted.

Separate recruitment from internship supervision. Refusal, low scores and withdrawal must not affect academic/internship standing. Use equal incentives, no forced purchases and no punitive withdrawal terms.

Use coded IDs, a separate protected linkage file, restricted access, minimum necessary financial data and a stated retention/deletion schedule. Vendors should not access unrelated banking history. Explain that the tests are research, not clinical diagnosis.

Keep the limitations tied to course terminology: external/population validity and generalizability; ecological validity and the Hawthorne effect; internal-validity threats from noncompliance, attrition, logging gaps and contamination. A payment-mode difference does not identify its mechanism. No clear difference must be interpreted using uncertainty, not as automatic equivalence.
